Aircraft Node · Kit 005 · Concept

ORION
AIR.

The aircraft node. Internet to the aircrew — for imagery uplink, onboard systems, and whatever comes next.

The core idea is simple: get internet to the aircrew. Whatever else the aircraft does — uploading imagery in flight, supporting onboard video gear, or running mission applications that need a connection — that all rides on this one foundation. Everything beyond that is conceptual and still being thought through.

Volunteers Serving America's CommunitiesU.S. Air Force Auxiliary
Concept Maturity

Anticipated, not committed.

ORION-Air is in the architecture because the design should anticipate it, not because it is ready to procure. Pricing is intentionally absent from the calculator. The kit will be specified once the questions below have answers.

What It Solves

Internet to the aircrew

The foundation is a working data connection in the cockpit. From there:

• Uploading imagery while the aircraft is still airborne, instead of waiting until landing
• Supporting on-board video or other systems that need internet access
• Potentially giving pilots real-time data when cellular coverage fails — though that use case still needs operational discussion
Service-Plan Speed

Starlink Mini in motion

Starlink Mini's in-motion service is rated up to roughly 480 km/h (about 300 mph). Typical CAP mission profiles — transit and grid-search alike — stay well below that ceiling, so the speed cap shouldn't constrain ORION-Air operation.

The remaining service-plan questions are about plan tier and cost, not airspeed.
Open Questions

Still being thought through

• How does this build out across the fleet of aircraft?
• What problem does it solve well enough to justify the integration cost?
• Mounting approach for each airframe, per airworthiness guidance
• Real-time-data-for-pilots use case — when is that the right answer vs. a cellular fallback?
Tracked alongside the architecture's other open design questions.
Status

Concept only. No procurement activity.

No procurement decisions, recurring service commitments, or airworthiness applications are in progress for ORION-Air. The architecture is documented so the kit can be specified rapidly once the operational questions have answers.
